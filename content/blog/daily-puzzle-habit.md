## Why Does Daily Practice Beat Cramming?

Chess improvement is pattern acquisition, and pattern memory consolidates between sessions, not during them. Solve a handful of puzzles today and your brain files those shapes overnight; meet a cousin of the same pattern tomorrow and the connection strengthens. Space the repetitions and the patterns stick for years. Cram them into one marathon and most of it washes out by next week.

That is why a genuinely daily habit — even a small one — compounds. One puzzle a day is 365 patterns a year filed into long-term memory.

## What Should a Daily Session Look Like?

Short, focused, and slightly varied. A good ten-minute template:

- Warm up with something fast and tactical — a [Mate in 1](/mate-in-1) or a [Takes](/takes) board.
- Do one "main course" at your level: [Mate in 2](/mate-in-2), [Check](/check), or a [Chess Solitaire](/chess-solitaire).
- Finish with something playful so the session ends on fun: a [Survival](/survival) run or a quick [Mini Chess](/minichess) game against the engine.

The variety matters. Mate puzzles train forcing calculation, capture puzzles train board vision, and endgame puzzles like [King and Pawn](/king-and-pawn) train precision. Rotating them builds a rounder game than hammering one category.

## Should You Solve Fast or Slow?

Both, on different days. Fast solving (a few seconds per easy puzzle) builds the instant recognition that carries real games. Slow solving (sitting with one hard [Mate in 3](/mate-in-3) until you see the whole line) builds calculation depth. A good rule: easy puzzles fast, hard puzzles honest — no moving until you have calculated the full sequence, and no undo as a crutch. Our scoring rewards exactly that: fewer undos and faster clean solves earn more points.

## What If You Get Stuck?

Being stuck is the productive part. The moment before you see the answer is when the pattern is being written. Resist the urge to reset immediately: name what is stopping you ("the king escapes to f7 every time"), then look for a piece that covers that exact square. If you truly hit a wall, step away — the answer has a habit of appearing the moment you stop forcing it.

## How Do You Keep the Streak Alive?

Anchor the puzzle to an existing habit — morning coffee, commute, lunch break. Keep the bar embarrassingly low: one puzzle counts. Streaks die from ambition ("I'll do ten every day") far more often than from laziness. And use the leaderboard as a nudge, not a judge: sign in, submit your scores, and let your [global ranking](/top-players) drift upward as the daily patterns pile up.
